Status: **CLOSED / PASS**

## Scope

This diagnostic isolates the 2048 -> 4096 tagged-table step under a fixed
12-bit index transform.

```text
physical e2048 -> canonical 12-bit hash, low 11 bits used
physical e4096 -> canonical 12-bit hash, all 12 bits used
```

PC mixing, folded-GHR index width, path-history mixing, history lengths
8/24/64, tag widths 8/9/10, table count, and all non-BPU architecture
controls remain fixed.

```text
BPU_B4_A1D_FIXED12_4096_NATIVE_CONVERGENCE=PASS
```

The fixed-12 e4096 point converged exactly to native e4096 on the validated

## Interpretation

The 2048 -> 4096 performance improvement persists under an invariant 12-bit
index transform and is slightly larger than in the native geometry sweep.

Therefore the evidence supports the scoped conclusion:

> On the frozen six-workload subset, the 2048 -> 4096 gain is not explained
> by favorable 11-to-12-bit native remapping. Increased tagged capacity and
> its downstream effects remain the dominant mechanism.

Mapping sensitivity is larger here than in the earlier fixed-11 diagnostic:

```text
e2048 fixed12/native geomean = +0.28086%
sglib-combined               = +1.081%
qrduino                       = +0.813%
```

Thus mapping remains measurable and workload-sensitive, but it is still much
smaller than the controlled capacity gains on the two hard workloads:

```text
sglib-combined  e2048 -> e4096 fixed12 = -6.731%
qrduino         e2048 -> e4096 fixed12 = -7.516%
```

Native-minus-controlled is not treated as an additive decomposition because
TAGE allocation/training evolves nonlinearly.
